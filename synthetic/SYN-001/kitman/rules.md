# Rules of the game: a plain-language guide

This is a short summary in our own words, written for fans in the stadium. It covers the rules most often asked about during a match. It is not the official text of the Laws of the Game.

## Match length and added time

- A match has two halves of 45 minutes, with a 15-minute half-time break.
- The referee adds time at the end of each half for time lost to substitutions, injuries, goal celebrations, VAR checks and time-wasting. The fourth official shows the minimum added time on a board.
- The clock does not stop when the ball goes out. The time the ball is actually in play is called *effective playing time*. It is usually between 50 and 60 minutes per match.

## Substitutions

- In the Meridian League each team names up to 9 substitutes and can make up to 5 substitutions.
- Substitutions can be made in at most 3 stoppages during play; changes made at half-time do not count as one of the 3.
- A player who has been substituted cannot come back on.

## Offside

- A player is in an offside position if, when a team-mate plays the ball, any part of their head, body or feet is in the opponents' half and closer to the goal line than both the ball and the second-last opponent. Hands and arms do not count.
- Being in an offside position is not an offence by itself. It becomes one only if the player then takes part in play: playing the ball, blocking an opponent's view or challenging an opponent for the ball.
- There is no offside from a goal kick, a throw-in or a corner kick.
- The assistant referee raises the flag; the referee decides.

## Fouls, free kicks and penalties

- A direct free kick is given for fouls such as tripping, pushing, holding, kicking an opponent or a deliberate handball. If it happens inside the offender's own penalty area, it is a penalty kick.
- An indirect free kick (the referee raises an arm) is given for other offences, such as dangerous play without contact or a goalkeeper picking up a deliberate back-pass from a team-mate's foot. A goal can be scored from it only after another player touches the ball.
- Opponents must stand at least 9.15 m from the ball at a free kick.

## Cards

- **Yellow card (caution)**: for offences such as unsporting behaviour, dissent, repeated fouls, delaying the restart or not respecting the distance.
- **Second yellow card**: the player is sent off (a red card follows).
- **Red card (sending-off)**: for serious foul play, violent conduct, spitting, denying an obvious goal-scoring opportunity with a foul or a handball, or offensive language. The team then plays with one player fewer.
- Substitutes on the bench and players who have already been substituted can also receive yellow and red cards. A substitute sent off before coming on cannot play, but the team still has 11 players on the pitch.
- Team officials (coaches and staff) can also receive yellow and red cards.

## Handball

- It is an offence when a player deliberately touches the ball with the hand or arm, or makes their body unnaturally bigger with the arm and the ball hits it.
- A goal scored directly with the hand or arm, even by accident, is not allowed.
- The goalkeeper can handle the ball inside their own penalty area.

## VAR (video assistant referee)

- The VAR team watches the match on screens and can help the referee only in four situations: goals, penalties, direct red cards and mistaken identity.
- The VAR can recommend an on-field review. The referee then watches the replay on the pitch-side monitor and makes the final decision.
- Factual decisions, like offside lines or whether the ball crossed the line, can be corrected by the VAR without an on-field review.
- At the Lanternworks Stadium, the big screens show a short message when a VAR check is in progress and when it is over.

## Restarts

- **Kick-off**: at the start of each half and after a goal, from the centre spot.
- **Throw-in**: when the ball crosses the touchline, taken with both hands from behind the head.
- **Goal kick**: when the attacking team last touched the ball before it crossed the goal line.
- **Corner kick**: when the defending team last touched the ball before it crossed its own goal line.
- **Dropped ball**: after the referee stops play without a foul, for example for an injury. It is given to the team that last touched the ball.

## When a match ends level

- In the league, a draw gives each team 1 point. A win gives 3 points; a defeat gives 0.
- Extra time and penalty shoot-outs are used only in cup knockout matches.
